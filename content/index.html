The business model

HR360 fills the gap a company has today and does not force a system replacement first. Commercial plans control access; the shared HR360 Web Platform and the HR360 Go mobile app deliver the experience.

HR360 Web PlatformShared admin and management environment; the interface adapts to subscription, entitlements, role and permissions.
HR360 GoOne adaptive mobile app: check-in with GPS, geofence, NFC or selfie; tasks; leave; payslips.
HR360 RecruitDedicated recruitment web app, standard and AI-assisted, converting candidates into employee profiles.
HR360 ConnectWorkforce messaging, calls and meetings. Roadmap; excluded from this plan's revenue.
EVE add-onsAI reporting and cross-module insights, sold as an add-on to HR360 Core clients from 2028.

Land and expand

Existing company systems→"What capability are we missing?"→Attendance / Tasks / Recruit→Add a second solution→HR360 Core + EVE

The model assumes 1% of Attendance and Tasks clients upgrade to HR360 Core every month (about 11% a year) and 1% of Attendance clients add Task Management every month. Upgrades are what make HR360 Core the largest revenue line even though it wins the fewest direct deals.

Buy the missing piece, not a new system

Most target companies already run an HR system, a payroll tool or a recruiting process. HR360 never asks them to switch. The buyer risks $15 to $100 a month and no migration, which is why the sale closes. HR360's own risk is volume: a low entry price means many pieces must be sold, which the sales targets below quantify.

Each pain answered by one plan

Attendance nobody trusts → Attendance Management

GPS, geofence, NFC or selfie check-in from HR360 Go; shifts, rules and a live who-is-in board; hours, overtime and lateness delivered payroll-ready. Approved locations and NFC points per site, rotating rosters and biometric device sync cover the field-team pain in the same plan.

Work lost in chat → Task Management

Tasks, projects, deadlines and a play/stop work timer; managers see workload and overdue work; employees get their list in HR360 Go.

Hiring by inbox → HR360 Recruit

Career pages, one pipeline, interview notes and scoring, standard or AI-assisted. A hired candidate becomes an employee profile with skills already recorded.

Everything connected → HR360 Core

Attendance feeds payroll, tasks feed performance, recruitment feeds the skills profile, and EVE answers questions across all of it. Leaves, requests and documents leave WhatsApp for one governed system.

Target market and ideal customer

Ideal customer profile

Companies with 50 to 200 employees that already run on spreadsheets, a legacy payroll tool or a punching machine, and are missing one capability: controlled attendance, task visibility, or structured hiring. Decision maker: owner, general manager or HR manager. Sales cycle: same-week for self-serve plans, four to eight weeks for HR360 Core.

Priority industries

Construction and contracting, retail and F&B chains, logistics, private schools and NGOs, clinics, and IT services. These have multi-branch or field workforces where geofenced and NFC attendance solves an immediate leakage problem and payroll-ready attendance saves HR days each month.

Markets

2027: Lebanon and Tunisia, where the team and the first clients are. 2028–2029: GCC entry (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait) through HR-consultant partners and remote self-serve sales, with on-ground presence following the first 20 GCC clients. 2030: broader Francophone Africa via Tunisian partners. Geographic timing is a management assumption.

Why HR360 wins in this segment

Global suites start at price points and implementation efforts built for 500+ employees. Local competitors sell attendance devices without software depth. HR360 lands at $15–$100 a month with a mobile-first app in Arabic, French and English, then grows into the full platform without a migration.

Marketing and go-to-market strategy

Paid ads are the primary engine from 2028; in 2027 the $1,000 cap means outbound calls, founder-led selling and email carry most of the load. HR consultancies distribute HR360 to their own client base for a 15% commission.

25% of new subscriptions in 2027 · 40% by 2030

Paid ads (primary engine)

  • Meta and Google search for Attendance and Task Management with self-serve checkout; LinkedIn for HR360 Core demos.
  • Landing page per plan with pricing, a 14-day trial and a demo booking form connected to the CRM.
  • Targets: 31 leads a month in 2027 rising to 267 in 2030 at $25 per lead and 9% lead-to-paid.
  • Budget: $800 a month in 2027 (inside the $1,000 marketing cap), $2,500 in 2028 and $12k in 2030.
40% of new subscriptions in 2027 · 25% by 2030

Cold calls and on-ground sales

  • Each rep works a named list of 50–200 employee companies: 150 calls a week, 8 visits a week, demo within 48 hours.
  • Quota: 2.5 closed deals per rep per month; 1.8 reps needed in 2027 (2 in the team, founders selling too), 6 by 2030 out of a sales team of 10.
  • Field reps carry NFC tags and a demo phone: the geofence check-in demo at the client's own site is the closing moment for Attendance.
  • HR360 Core proposals use the existing proposal template with a free basic customisation during onboarding.
10% growing to 25%

Partnerships with HR consultancies

  • HR consulting and payroll-outsourcing firms recommend HR360 to their clients and can run onboarding themselves.
  • Commission: 15% of the revenue they source, for the life of the client; partner dashboard and co-branded materials.
  • Targets: 1 active partners in 2027, 4 in 2028, 8 in 2029 and 15 in 2030 at one deal per partner per month.
  • Partners are the entry route to GCC markets before HR360 has staff on the ground.
25% of new subscriptions in 2027 · 10% by 2030

Email marketing

  • Automated sequences for trial users, demo no-shows and lapsed leads; monthly educational newsletter on attendance policy, payroll and hiring.
  • Feature launches (Recruit AI, EVE, MCP connectivity when live) announced to the whole base as upsell campaigns.
  • Targets: 69 engaged leads a month in 2027 at 4% conversion; the cheapest channel, so it is over-weighted in the lean year.
  • Re-marketing audiences from the CRM feed back into paid ads.

Risks and mitigations

Volume risk from low entry prices

Plans start at $15 a month, so the model needs 11 new subscriptions a month in 2027 and 60 by 2030 rather than a few large contracts. Mitigation: self-serve checkout so volume does not need sales hours, cross-sell and upgrades that raise revenue per client over time, and HR360 Core demos for the companies that want one system from day one.

Self-serve conversion lower than 9%

Every point of conversion lost raises CAC by about $30. Mitigation: free 14-day trial, onboarding videos, WhatsApp support, and partner-led installs for Attendance.

2027 sales capacity

Two sales people and $1,000 of marketing must deliver 11 new subscriptions a month. Mitigation: founders sell alongside the reps, 150 calls a week per rep, and referral incentives for the first clients.

Payment collection in Lebanon and Tunisia

Card penetration is uneven. Mitigation: annual invoicing with the 2-months-free incentive, bank transfer, and partner billing in GCC.

Upgrade rate to HR360 Core below plan
